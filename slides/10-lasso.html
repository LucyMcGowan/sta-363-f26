Review

What is ridge regression minimizing, and what is \(\hat\beta^R\)?

RSS \(+ \lambda\sum_{j=1}^p\beta_j^2\), minimized by \((\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X} + \lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{y}\).

What ridge cannot do

Ridge keeps all \(p\) predictors in the model at every finite \(\lambda\). On Credit that is ten coefficients to report when four of them carry the signal.

Subset selection drops predictors and ridge shrinks them. Is there something that does both?

The lasso

The lasso

\[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jx_{ij}\right)^2 + \lambda\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j|\]

The lasso

\[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jx_{ij}\right)^2 + \lambda\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$|\beta_j|$}}\]

The absolute value of one coefficient, where ridge had its square. That is the whole difference between the two methods.

The lasso

\[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jx_{ij}\right)^2 + \lambda{\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j|$}}\]

Summed over the \(p\) predictors, this is the \(\ell_1\) norm \(\|\beta\|_1\), where ridge used the squared \(\ell_2\) norm \(\|\beta\|_2^2\). The sum starts at \(j = 1\), so the intercept goes unpenalized here too.

The lasso

\[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jx_{ij}\right)^2 + {\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$\lambda$}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j|\]

The same tuning parameter, chosen the same way, by cross-validation. At \(\lambda = 0\) this is least squares, and at \(\lambda\) large enough every coefficient is exactly zero.

Two norms

Ridge

\[\|\beta\|_2^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2\]

Shrinks every coefficient toward zero.

Lasso

\[\|\beta\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j|\]

Shrinks, and sets some to exactly zero.

A model with some coefficients exactly zero is called sparse. The lasso performs variable selection as a side effect of shrinking.

Why the lasso zeroes out

The budget form

Lasso

minimize RSS subject to \[\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j| \leq s\]

Ridge

minimize RSS subject to \[\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2 \leq s\]

For every \(\lambda\) there is an \(s\) giving the same answer. Large \(\lambda\) is a small budget.

The constraint regions

ISLP Figure 6.7. Contours of RSS in red against the constraint regions in blue: a diamond for the lasso on the left, a circle for ridge on the right.

Corners

The RSS contours grow outward from \(\hat\beta\) until they touch the constraint region. The diamond has corners on the axes, and a corner is a point where one coefficient is exactly zero.

A circle has no corners. Where does the ridge solution touch, and what does that mean for a coefficient reaching zero?

Best subset in the same picture

Best subset selection is minimizing RSS subject to \(\sum_{j=1}^{p}I(\beta_j \neq 0) \leq s\): a budget on how many coefficients may be nonzero.

Same shape of problem, a budget that cannot be searched in reasonable time. The lasso replaces it with one that can.

Elastic net

Elastic net

\[\text{RSS} + \lambda\left(\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j| + (1-\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2\right)\]

Elastic net

\[\text{RSS} + \lambda\left({\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j|$}} + (1-\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2\right)\]

The lasso part of the penalty, which is what drives coefficients to exactly zero and makes the fit sparse.

Elastic net

\[\text{RSS} + \lambda\left(\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j| + {\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$(1-\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2$}}\right)\]

The ridge part, which shrinks everything and handles correlated predictors without having to choose between them.

Elastic net

\[\text{RSS} + \lambda\left({\require{color}\colorbox{#eec8e1}{$\alpha$}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\beta_j| + (1-\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j^2\right)\]

The mixing parameter, between 0 and 1. At \(\alpha = 1\) this is the lasso and at \(\alpha = 0\) it is ridge. It is scikit-learn’s l1_ratio, and it is tuned by cross-validation alongside \(\lambda\).

When mixing helps

  • The lasso picks one of a group of correlated predictors and drops the rest
  • When \(p > n\) the lasso can select at most \(n\) predictors
  • The ridge part lifts both limits while the lasso part keeps the sparsity

In Python

Lasso in scikit-learn

DATA = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LucyMcGowan/sta-363-f26/main/data/"
from sklearn.linear_model import Lasso
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline

Hitters = pd.read_csv(DATA + "Hitters.csv").dropna(subset=["Salary"])
X = pd.get_dummies(Hitters.drop(columns=["Salary"]), drop_first=True).astype(float)
y = Hitters["Salary"]

fit = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), Lasso(alpha=70, max_iter=200000)).fit(X, y)
pd.Series(fit[-1].coef_, index=X.columns).loc[lambda s: s != 0].round(1)

since the lasso has no closed form the way ridge does and small alpha values converge slowly.
3
.loc[lambda s: s != 0] keeps only the nonzero coefficients, which is the

Tuning alpha

from sklearn.model_selection import GridSearchCV, KFold

cv = KFold(n_splits=10, shuffle=True, random_state=363)
grid = GridSearchCV(
    make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), Lasso(max_iter=200000)),
    {"lasso__alpha": np.logspace(-1, 3, 60)},
    cv=cv, scoring="neg_mean_squared_error",
).fit(X, y)

kept = int((grid.best_estimator_[-1].coef_ != 0).sum())
round(grid.best_params_["lasso__alpha"], 2), round(-grid.best_score_), kept
1
Sixty candidate penalties on a log scale from 0.1 to 1000, reaching the
alpha of the pipeline step named lasso.
2
grid.best_estimator_ is the pipeline refit on all the data at the winning
alpha, so counting its nonzero coefficients says how many predictors the chosen model actually uses. 
(np.float64(1.22), 114828, 17)

The winning alpha keeps 17 of 19. Where did the sparsity go?
