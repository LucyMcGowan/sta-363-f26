Policies

This syllabus and the dates are subject to change.

Class

This class is meant to be hands on. You are responsible for the assigned reading before each class. Please bring a charged laptop.

The rhythm is the same most weeks.

Wednesday and Friday a new method, then an application exercise using it
Most Wednesdays a short paper check-in at the start of class
Most Fridays a problem set due

Attendance

If you feel unwell please do not come to class. All class material is posted, and I am happy to meet outside class to help you catch up.

Computing

We use Python in Google Colab, so there is nothing to install. See Getting Started with Colab.

Course components

Application exercises

Done in class, in pairs or small groups. Graded for completion.

Problem sets

Roughly eleven across the semester, due Fridays.

You may work together, however I want you to write up your own answers. If you work with other classmates please note that on your problem set.

Check-ins

Roughly ten short check-ins at the start of class.

Exams

We will have two midterm exams and a final exam. Exams will be in class and closed book.

Late and missed work

  • Problem sets, late but within 24 hours of the due time: 50% credit
  • Any later: no credit
  • Check-ins cannot be made up, which is why the lowest two are dropped
  • No make-up exams

All regrade requests must be raised with me within one week of receiving the grade. ## Accessibility

Wake Forest University provides reasonable accommodations for students with disabilities. Academic accommodations are coordinated through the Center for Learning, Access, and Student Success (CLASS). If you would like to request accommodations for this course, contact CLASS as early in the semester as possible (class.wfu.edu, 118 Reynolda Hall).

Honor code

Wake Forest University is committed to a culture of academic integrity. As a part of this community, you share the responsibility for creating a place of honesty, intellectual curiosity, and individual accountability. As you committed to with your honor pledge signature, you agree “not to deceive any member of the community; not to steal, cheat, or plagiarize on academic work; and not to engage in any other form of academic misconduct.” If you have questions about documenting your work, working with external sources, or working with peers on assigned work, consult with me as soon as possible. Instances of academic dishonesty will be referred to the Honor and Ethics Council.

Sharing code and responses

On problem sets you may use online resources, including StackOverflow and AI assistants, and you must cite any borrowed or adapted code, whether copied directly or modified. Uncited reuse is treated as plagiarism.

On application exercises, use whatever resources you like.

Check-ins and exams are closed book and closed device.

Rather than relying on someone else’s work, ask for help. See Help.

Getting help

See the Help page for office hours, the Math & Stats Center.
